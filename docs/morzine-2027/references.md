## 1. Race A — RunThrough Trails Morzine-Avoriaz 100K

### 1.1 Identity of the event
- It is a **new event, first held in June 2025**, run by RunThrough Trails (a UK organiser). It is listed as "Ultra Trail Morzine-Avoriaz" on ITRA and as "RunThrough Trails Ultra Trail Morzine-Avoriaz – Morzine 100K" on UTMB Index (race id 54704). It is a UTMB Index race (100K category).
- It is **not** the Trail des Hauts Forts. That is a different, older race in Morzine, held in August (52 km / ~3,800 m, plus Trail du Fornet 36 km and Mines d'Or). It is not a predecessor and has no 100 km. Sources: https://utmb.world/utmb-index/races/647.traildeshautsfortstraildeshautsforts.2025 , https://www.betrail.run/fr/race/trail.des.hauts.forts/2025/overview
- So only **two editions of results** exist (2025, 2026).

**Route character (organiser):** a loop from Morzine (Place du Baraty) over the east side of the Morzine valley and through Avoriaz, in the Portes du Soleil. It mixes village trails, beech/fir forest, lakesides and open terrain above the treeline, with "fast-flowing tracks, technical climbs and descents". The high point is about 2,200 m, so it is a mid-altitude race and altitude is not a factor. No public source names the cols. The likely high ground is the Avoriaz / Hauts-Forts / Chavanette–Coux–Chésery area, but this is **unverified**.

**Profile implication:** with a longest climb of ~1,170 m and a longest descent of ~730 m (2025 GPX), the race is a sawtooth of many medium climbs, roughly 8–10 climbs of 400–1,100 m. It is not a few huge cols. Descents are numerous, so quad durability matters more than climbing VAM. That inference comes from the per-climb maxima against the total D+.

## 2. Race B — Ultra Boucle de la Sarra, 6 h relay (Lyon, Fourvière)

### 2.1 Format and rules (règlement, 2027)
- **Teams of 2 or 3, ranked together** (there is no separate duo ranking). Men's/mixed and women's podiums are awarded. Minimum age 18. The 6 h relay is capped at 50 teams.
- **Relay order is free.** Runners organise their own rotation, and there is no rule of one loop each in turn. The handover is in a designated zone after the timing arch, and only the runner carrying the relay baton may pass under the arch.
- **A loop started before 6:00:00 counts.** No loop may start after 6 h. Teams are ranked by loops, then by time. That is why winning teams' times are 6:05–6:20.
- The loop has aid stations (food and drink), and a head torch is mandatory (start at 17:00, finish around 23:00). **Poles are banned.**
- Start: **Saturday 22/05/2027, 17:00.**
- Source: https://www.ultraboucledelasarra.com/reglement/

### 2.2 Loop
- Official description: ~2.2 km and ~90 m D+. Timing distance is **2.09 km per loop** (73.15 km / 35 loops). The 2026 athle.fr listing says 2,040 m.
- Surfaces: the grassy Piste de la Sarra slope over dirt, the Chemin de Montauban (asphalt and cobbles, twisty, narrow), the **Montée Nicolas de Lange (563 stone steps)**, and the stabilised Chemin du Viaduc.
- Source: https://www.ultraboucledelasarra.com/parcours/

### 2.4 Loop-time arithmetic
For N loops, the N-th loop must start before 6:00:00. The team therefore needs (N−1) loops, including handovers, in under 360 min.

| Loops | Max avg loop incl. handover | Speed equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| 36 | 10:17 | 12.2 km/h |
| 35 | 10:35 | 11.9 km/h |
| 34 | 10:54 | 11.5 km/h |
| 33 | 11:15 | 11.1 km/h |
| 32 | 11:37 | 10.8 km/h |
| 30 | 12:25 | 10.1 km/h |
| 27 | 13:51 | 9.1 km/h |

In a duo, each runner does about 17–18 loops with a teammate's loop (~10–11 min) as rest. That makes it an **18 × ~10.5 min on / ~10.5 min off session** over 6 h, run in the evening on stairs and cobbles.

Physiological translation (estimate): with ~90 m D+ counting for roughly 8 m of flat running per metre climbed, one loop ≈ 2.8 km flat-equivalent.
- An 11:15 loop needs ~15 km/h flat-equivalent, about 105–110 % of a 14 km/h critical speed. That is sustainable only because of the 1:1 recovery, and only if the descent and stair technique are efficient.
- A 10:35 loop (35 loops) needs ~16 km/h flat-equivalent, about 115 % CS.
- These are estimates, not measured.

## 3. Training of amateurs who reach top 5–10 % on 100 km / ~5,800 m mountain races

**Direct evidence is thin.** No public dataset links finish percentile to training on a race like this one. What exists:

- **Strava, UTMB finishers (all levels):**
  - Over 24 weeks, an average of ~6 h/week (61 km, ~1,500 m D+ on average).
  - Peak week averages ~127 km and ~4,900 m D+ (interquartile range ~106–156 km), usually 3 weeks before the race.
  - 62 % did at least one run over 80 km, and 57 % raced in the build.
  - There was no significant training difference between finishers and DNFs.
  - Source: https://www.outsideonline.com/running/strava-data-utmb/?scope=anon
- **Koop (CTS) "minimum-maximum" for a 100K:** at least 9 h/week for at least 6 consecutive weeks, starting about 9 weeks out. It is a floor; protect the long run. Source: https://trainright.com/ultramarathon-training-time-required/
- **Peer-reviewed (Spanish mountain ultras, amateur field):** 10 ± 4 h/week and 1,772 ± 691 m D+/week for ultra finishers. Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0238846&type=printable
- **Elite 100 km (road):** weekly km was an independent predictor of performance. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33745586/
- **Plans aimed at competitive amateurs:**
  - A competitive mountain-100K plan goes from 65–70 km to ~115 km/week: https://www.trainingpeaks.com/training-plans/running/ultra/tp-397487/24-week-mountain-100km-plan-competitive
  - The Uphill Athlete 100K plan peaks at ~66 km and ~4,000 m D+/week: https://uphillathlete.com/?p=86169
  - Arduua's UTMB-100K intermediate plan uses 8–12 h/week.
- **Muscular endurance (Uphill Athlete):**
  - Weighted steep carries or step-ups for climbing, plus *separate* eccentric step-down and downhill work. Climbing-only ME leaves "legs that fall apart on descents".
  - Once a week is enough; gains plateau after 8–16 weeks; never cut aerobic volume to make room for it.
  - Source: https://uphillathlete.com/strength-training/muscular-endurance-for-mountain-athletes/
- **Downhill durability:** one damaging downhill bout protects against muscle damage, soreness and loss of running mechanics in later bouts (repeated-bout effect). The protection lasts weeks, possibly months, so regular downhill exposure is the main lever for a weak descender.
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9189713
  - https://air.unimi.it/bitstream/2434/919482/2/Khassetarash%20et%20al_The%20repeated%20bout%20effect%20influences%20lower-extremity%20biomechanics%20during%20a%2030-min%20downhill%20run.pdf
  - https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/24976/8/Muscle%20soreness%20but%20not%20neuromuscular%20fatigue%20responses%20following%20downhill%20running%20differ%20according%20to%20the%20number%20of%20exercise%20bouts.pdf

**Synthesis (my inference, not a measured dataset):** amateurs at UTMB 100K index ~620–680 with jobs typically show the following in the 6 months before a race like this.
- **Volume:** a base of 8–10 h/week, then **11–14 h/week for 6–9 weeks** (peaking about 3–5 weeks out). That is ~70–110 km/week and **3,000–5,500 m D+/week** in the specific block.
- **Long days:**
  - 5–7 h long days with 2,000–3,000 m D+, at least 3–4 of them in the last 10 weeks.
  - At least one back-to-back weekend: for example 6 h + 3–4 h, or about 4,000–5,000 m over two days.
  - One rehearsal race of 50–70 km / 3,000–4,000 m roughly 5–8 weeks before the race.
- **Intensity:**
  - 1 session/week of uphill threshold or VO2 work (e.g. 5–6 × 5–8 min uphill, or 3 × 15–20 min tempo climbs).
  - 1 ME / strength session.
  - The rest easy (75–85 % of time).
  - Descents trained on purpose: 1–2 sessions/week including 500–1,000 m of fast technical downhill, done consistently and not in a cram.
- **Taper:** about 2–3 weeks.

The athlete's own limiters are descending, durability and a 6-week break. In that profile, the gap to top 10 % usually opens in the last 40 km, not on the climbs. The highest-return items are therefore downhill volume and long days, not VO2max.

**Interpreting VO2max ~57 / CS ~14 km/h (low confidence):**
- On flat equivalence, that physiology corresponds to a ~41–42 min 10 km. Runners with that profile often score UTMB index ~600–680 on short trails.
- On 100K mountain races they score lower unless durability and descending are trained (often 30–80 points lower).
- So top 10 % (index ~620) looks physiologically reachable. Top 5 % (index ~665) needs the durability gap closed almost entirely.

I found no public podcast or blog that documents an athlete with this exact profile, a 6-month block and a top-10 % finish at this race. The French searches (DLTDC, Trail Session, Ausha catalogues) returned only generic plans.
