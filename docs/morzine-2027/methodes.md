# 29-week preparation for a 100 km Alpine ultra (97 km / 5,700 m D+, 19/06/2027), with a 6 h relay on 22/05/2027

The athlete: male, 31, VO2max ~57–58 (lab test from July 2025, taken before the break), CS 14.0–14.6 km/h, VT1 155 / VT2 171 / HRmax 190. His measured limiter is durability: efficiency drops 4.4 %/h. His descending is weak and his climbing good. He is coming back from 6 weeks off (elbow), restarted on 09/11, and his calves are sore. He has 8–14 h/week, one Alpine weekend a month and lives in Lyon. He wants **running sessions only**.

One caveat up front. Heavy strength training is the best-supported way to improve durability. Strength and plyometric RCTs reduced the rise in O2 cost over 90 min and improved fatigued performance (see Born on the Trail's review, below). Leaving it out is the athlete's choice. This report uses running-only substitutes instead: hill sprints, steep power-hike ME, stair repeats and downhill eccentric work. The evidence for these is weaker for durability specifically.

## 1. Periodization models

### Jason Koop (CTS, *Training Essentials for Ultrarunning*): reverse periodization
- **Principle:** for ultras, long low-intensity running is the most race-specific stimulus. Short, high-intensity work is the least specific, so it comes first. Specific volume (back-to-backs, long mountain days) builds through the final blocks before the event ([Born on the Trail on Koop](https://bornonthetrail.substack.com/p/how-elite-coaches-structure-winter-training); [TrainRight](https://trainright.com/crush-ultramarathon-training-goals/)).
- **Workout families** ([TrainRight: decoding intervals](https://trainright.com/decoding-ultramarathon-interval-workouts/); [key workouts](https://trainright.com/key-workouts-every-ultrarunner-should-do/)):
  - **RunningIntervals (VO2):** 2–4 min reps at RPE 9–10, 12–24 min total, 1:1 recovery. Uphill preferred, never downhill. Example: 6×3 min / 3 min inside a 90 min run. 4×3 min beats 12×1 min or 2×6 min.
  - **TempoRun (threshold):** 8–15 (up to 20) min reps at RPE 8–9, 30–60 min total (up to 90 per CTS), recovery half the rep length. Example: 3×12 min / 6 min inside a 2 h run.
  - **SteadyStateRun:** 20–60 min reps at RPE 7, 30 min–2 h total, work:rest 5–8:1. Example: 2×30 min / 5 min.
  - Intervals sit *inside* an endurance run.
- **Sequence:** VO2 block → tempo block → steady state plus specific long runs. Each quality type is concentrated in its own block, and the others are maintained.
- **Supporting evidence:** long VO2 intervals accumulate more time above 90 % VO2max than "intensified" short ones ([PMC11743937](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11743937/); KoopCast #198 with M. Rosenblat).
- **Return from a break:** easy 45–60 min runs at RPE 4–5. Someone who kept ≥ 50 % of volume gets back to 70 % in 2–3 weeks, versus 4–6 weeks after stopping completely.

### Uphill Athlete (Scott Johnston, Kilian Jornet, Steve House)
Sources: [UA trail training](https://uphillathlete.com/trail-running/training-for-trail-running/), [ME article](https://uphillathlete.com/strength-training/muscular-endurance-for-mountain-athletes/), [100 km plan](https://uphillathlete.com/training-plans/100-kilometer-mountain-ultra/).

**Load and intensity distribution**
- ≥ 90 % of time in Z1–Z2. ≤ 10 % high intensity, counted as time at intensity only.
- Volume and intensity never rise in the same week. Each rises by ≤ 10 %/week.
- 3:1 build/consolidation cycles. Deload weeks keep the same structure at up to −50 % volume.

**Base period** (months out from the A race)
- One power session per week: hill sprints of 8–10 s at max effort on ≥ 20 % (or stairs), 2–3 min recovery, starting at 6–8 reps. Stop when distance drops.
- One ME session per week.

**Z3/Z4 sessions**
- Z3: continuous, or reps with 2–4 min recovery, 30–60 min total.
  - Beginner: 2×15 min. Advanced: 3×20 min.
- Z4: 3–8 min reps, 16–24 min total.
  - Beginner: 3×3 min / 3 min. Advanced: 4×8 min / 3 min.
- Use a grade that matches the race.
- Z3 work comes first, and Z4 replaces it gradually: 2 min of Z3 → 1 min of Z4.
- Start high intensity at about 5 % of weekly volume and grow toward 10 %. Hard sessions are ≥ 48 h apart, 72 h preferred.

**Muscular endurance (ME)**
- A *phase* of 8–16 weeks, once a week, in late base or the specific period.
- Main form: steep weighted uphill carry. Legs burn but conversation stays possible. If breathing is the limiter, the slope is too gentle.
- Unweighted Z3/Z4 hill work and high-volume mountain running also count as ME. The latter is the most taxing.
- Include both concentric (uphill) and eccentric (downhill) work.

**Transition (2–3 months out) for long races**
- ME stops. Hill sprints continue. Back-to-back long runs and one Z3 or Z4 session per week are added. One downhill interval per week on race-like terrain.

**Taper**
- 2 weeks for races under 100 miles. About −25 % hours per week. Speed work is cut half as much as overall volume.

**The 100 km plan**
- 20 weeks plus a 2-week taper. Starts with hill sprints, then 10 weeks of ME, 3:1 blocks and progressive weekend long runs.
- A 3-day run sequence of increasing length serves as the durability and fueling rehearsal.
- Peak week ≈ 66 km with > 4,000 m combined gain and loss. Note how *modest* that peak is.

**Kilian's own data**
- ~1,000–1,200 h/year over ~400 sessions, about 90 % endurance. His 2022 distribution was 58/19/16/4/3 % across Z1–Z5, which is pyramidal ([mtnath](https://mtnath.com/kilians-jornet-training/); [Trail Runner, Kilian 2022 data](https://www.trailrunnermag.com/training/trail-tips-training/kilian-jornet-training-data/)).
- Weeks run 15–35 h, often with double days.

### David Roche (SWAP)
Sources: [Born on the Trail](https://bornonthetrail.substack.com/p/how-elite-coaches-structure-winter-training); [Scientific Triathlon interview](https://scientifictriathlon.com/tts444/). The Trail Runner pieces are paywalled; their details below come from search summaries.
- **Base:** almost everything below AeT. Progress frequency first (4–6 runs/week), then duration, then one long run.
- **Hill strides:** after several weeks of consistent frequency, add 4–8 × 15–30 s uphill strides, 1–3×/week, in the second half of easy runs. Flat strides come later. Roche calls hill strides the best strength training for runners and does them 3–4×/week himself.
- Progression runs of 15–40 min, done only "on days you feel responsive".
- Hill tempos sit alongside long runs.
- **"Hill Beast":** 10/8/6/4/2 min climbs with run-down recovery.
- **Philosophy:** consistency and enjoyment over perfect terrain, with the emphasis on quality and recovery rather than raw volume.

### Norwegian (double) threshold, adapted
- The model controls intensity by lactate (≈ 2–4 mmol/L) and stays *below* LT2. That is roughly 85–90 % HRmax, or for this athlete ~160–167 bpm (94–98 % VT2) ([Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/double-threshold-training/); [o2trening](https://o2trening.substack.com/p/the-norwegian-method-for-recreational)).
- **For trail athletes**, coaches reduce it to 15–30 min of threshold per AM/PM session, used *sporadically* in blocks ([Trail Runner overview](https://www.trailrunnermag.com/training/trail-tips-training/an-overview-of-the-norwegian-approach-to-running-training/)).
- **For amateurs**, the realistic version is "singles": one sub-threshold session about every other day.
- **Verdict:** at 8–14 h with weekly long mountain days, doubles make no sense. Use *one* controlled sub-threshold session a week (flat on the Rhône/Saône quays, or a gentle grade) in Build 2. It is low-damage, high-volume quality that lifts CS without the muscle cost of VO2 hill work.

### Guillaume Millet and colleagues (Saint-Étienne): fatigue in mountain ultras
- **Size of the damage:** an extreme mountain ultra reduced knee-extensor and plantar-flexor MVC by −35 % and −39 %, with a large central component (voluntary activation −19 %) ([PLoS ONE 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0017059)).
- **Longer and slower means less damage:** at the 330 km Tor des Géants, MVC fell only −24 % / −26 % and CK was ~3,700 vs ~13,600 U/L after UTMB ([PLoS ONE 2013](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0065596)). Pacing intensity drives the damage.
- **The "Flush model":** neuromuscular fatigue, especially eccentric damage on descents, regulates ultra pace ([Millet 2011](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51167760_Can_Neuromuscular_Fatigue_Explain_Running_Strategies_and_Performance_in_Ultra-Marathons_The_Flush_Model)).
- **What this means here:** for a 97 km / 5,700 m race, the quads (about 5,700 m D−) and calves limit performance more than VO2max does. This matches the athlete's durability and descent limiters.
- A finding from the same tradition: an extreme ultra *lowered* the energy cost of uphill walking and running ([PMC5100553](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5100553/)). Fatigue changes how he moves.
- **Practical advice** (book *Ultra-trail : plaisir, performance et santé*): adjust poles to about a 90° elbow and train with race poles; alternate sweet and salty food from the start ([club cardio sport dossier](https://www.clubcardiosport.com/sites/www.clubcardiosport.com/files/userfiles/L%E2%80%99ultra-endurance.pdf); [Unlimited Miles notes](https://www.unlimitedmiles.net/2024/11/chapitre-6-finaliser-gerer-un-ultra-trail/)).
- Millet's "0 to 100" project brings sedentary adults to 100 km on 10–15 h/week.

### French practice (RunMotion and others)
- **Phases:** 16–24 weeks of specific prep. Foncier (W1–8, short hills, 2h30–3h long runs), then spécifique (W9–17, long hills such as 4×8 min, footing with 10×1 min descents, 4–6 h rando-course, sometimes over 2 days), then a 3-week affûtage at −30–50 %.
- **Key numbers:** 2–3 night runs. Peak amateur load is 7–12 h/week. The longest days (5–6 h, or a "week-end choc") fall 4–6 weeks out. 100 m D+ ≈ 1 km-effort ([RunMotion](https://run-motion.com/plan-entrainement-ultra-trail-100km/)).
- **Descent block** ([altitude-trail](https://www.altitude-trail.fr/articles/entrainement-specifique-descente-trail-excentrique)):
  - 30–60 min continuous descent at 8–12 % inside the long run, plus 6×200 m fast technical descents.
  - Weekly D− rises ≤ 10 %, with the block 6–10 weeks out.
  - No "crash" descents in the last 2 weeks (e.g. never 3,000 m D− two weeks out).

### Elite weeks, scaled down
- **Jim Walmsley:** ~18 h, ~200 km and ~6,000 m D+ per week around his 50-mile record. Peak WSER block 190/170/190 km, with one week > 6,700 m. All singles. He trains slower and more vertical than the course, and his longest run was about 9 h ([iRunFar 2026](https://www.irunfar.com/jim-walmsley-pre-2026-western-states-100-interview); [Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/ie-en/jim-walmsley-record-50-miles-run)).
- **François D'Haene:** counts time, not kilometres. Only 2–3 big weeks a year (30–35 h, including hiking and cycling), each about a month before a major goal. Blocks are separated by a very light week ([trakks](https://www.trakks.be/en/news/francois-dhaene-il-faut-toujours-rester-humble-face-lultra-trail); [u-Trail](https://www2.u-trail.com/entrainement-haene/)).
- **Scaling to 8–14 h/week (about 50–70 % of elite hours):**
  - Keep the elite *pattern*: time-based planning, ≥ 80 % easy, one or two hard sessions plus long days.
  - Make one "big week" around 5–6 weeks out (Ascension weekend, W23).
  - Train more vertical than the race per hour (race ≈ 59 m D+/km). Accept fewer total kilometres.

## 2. Durability: why his −4.4 %/h is the main target

- **Volume and long-run habit protect economy.** Runners on ~92 km/week saw O2 cost rise 2.4 % over 90 min, versus 4.5 % on ~43 km/week. Runners used to runs > 90 min rose 3.1 %, versus 6.0 % for others, with VO2max and 10 km performance matched (Zanini et al., in [Born on the Trail](https://bornonthetrail.substack.com/p/how-to-run-faster-further-into-your)). His 4.4 %/h is in the *poorly conditioned* range.
- **Fresh lab values don't predict the fade.** After 2 h of heavy exercise, CP fell ~8 % and W′ ~20%, and glycogen did not explain it (Clark 2018/2019). In pooled data, CP losses ranged from < 1 % to about 33 %, and fresh VO2max didn't predict who lost what (Jones 2024).
- **Trail fade is muscular, especially uphill.** In 90 min of trail running, TT slowed 7.7 %, mostly on the climbs, with *no* change in O2 cost. A 109 min simulated race cut quad force by 11.9 %, which tracked the speed loss. Over 3 h at sub-threshold, uphill TT distance fell 6.6 % (Lloria-Varella; Jaén-Carrillo).
- **Concept:** durability is the "fourth determinant", and there is no standard measurement yet ([Maunder et al. 2021](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/351064181_The_Importance_of_'Durability'_in_the_Physiological_Profiling_of_Endurance_Athletes); [Experimental Physiology 2025](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Durability-as-an-index-of-endurance-exercise-performance.pdf)). Two hours of moderate running also lowers the speed at the moderate–heavy boundary ([PMC12479608](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12479608/)).
- **Running-only levers, in rough order of evidence:**
  1. Consistent weekly volume, with long runs > 2–3 h every week from January.
  2. Quality placed late in long runs (steady-state or climbs in the final hour), as the "fatigued quality" stimulus.
  3. Fueling at 60–90 g/h during long sessions.
  4. Downhill and eccentric conditioning (repeated bout effect).
  5. Steep power-hike ME.
  6. Back-to-back days.
- **Retest** with the same 3 h protocol in W12, W20 and W24 to see whether the slope moves.

Two predictor studies frame the priorities:
- In 45–90 km mountain races, training volume and intensity were the main predictors, and higher weekly altitude gain was associated with better results ([Belinchón-deMiguel 2021, PMC7908619](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7908619/)).
- In a 65 km / 4,000 m race, lab VO2max and peak power correlated best with time ([Fornasiero 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28869746/)).

So both the engine and the volume/vert matter.

## 3. Quality sessions: evidence and progression (summary; full prescriptions in the Session library)

- **Uphill VO2max repeats.** Koop: 2–4 min, 12–24 min total, 1:1, uphill. Long reps give more time > 90 % VO2max than short "intensified" ones. Uphill gives the intensity with far less impact.
  - Progression: 5×2 min → 6×3 min → 5×4 min → 6×4 min (W5–W12).
  - Then maintenance every 10–14 days, then a short sharpener in the taper.
- **Threshold (flat and uphill).**
  - Uphill progression: 3×8 min → 3×12 min → 2×20 min, then 3×15 min on a race-like grade (W9–W20).
  - Flat sub-threshold "singles" in Build 2: 6×6 min → 3×12 min at 95–98 % VT2.
  - Limit total Z4 to 30–40 min per session (16–24 min in pure Z4 per UA).
- **Steady-state long climbs** (Koop SSR / UA Z3): continuous 30–60 min climbs at 91–95 % VT2, building to 2×30–45 min in Apr–May. These are the most race-relevant intensity for the 100 km climbs at the first half of the race, and they double as ME.
- **Downhill / eccentric conditioning.**
  - Easthope et al.: four weekly trail bouts cut post-run strength loss from about 18 % to about 3 %.
  - A second 30 min downhill run 3 weeks later produced smaller MVIC loss, less pain and attenuated biomechanical changes ([PubMed 35225166](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35225166/)).
  - Trail runners doing downhill reps ≥ 1×/week had lower CK (182 vs 290 U/L) and kept squat strength (+4 % vs −9.1 %), while strength training alone made no difference. That study is exploratory, n = 36 ([PMC12846201](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12846201/)).
  - The repeated bout effect may last up to ~9 weeks for some markers and is gone by 12 weeks (non-running data). Practitioners say a long descent every 10–14 days is needed at peak ([iRunFar](https://www.irunfar.com/sprint-based-hill-and-predictor-workouts-for-ultramarathon-training)).
  - The RBE reduces soreness but not all neuromuscular fatigue. Braking forces at −9° are +73 % versus flat. Allow ≥ 48 h after a hard downhill session. Keep cadence within ±5 % of preferred ([review PMC12592170](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12592170/)).
  - Progression: grade → duration → speed, one variable at a time.
- **Muscular endurance (steep hikes).** Once a week for 8–16 weeks (here W9–W20).
  - Start unweighted on 20–35 % trails or stairs. Optionally add 5–10 % of body weight in a pack from W13. Legs are the limiter, not breathing.
  - Drop the pack and keep only steep Z3 climbs in the 8 weeks before the race (UA transition rule).
- **Power hiking and poles.**
  - Above ~15 % grade, the energy cost of walking and running both rise roughly in proportion to slope (Minetti). The preferred walk–run switch on steep inclines (15°) minimizes energy, and at a fixed 800 m/h it matches the energetic crossover ([JEB 2021](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/224/3/jeb233056/223392/Does-the-preferred-walk-run-transition-speed-on); [EJAP 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42530641/)).
  - Rule: run below ~12–15 %, hike above ~18–20 %, and practise both.
  - Poles: about 2.5 % faster on a ~19° climb but not cheaper (Giovanelli 2022). Lower RPE above about 20°. *More* economical than no poles after a simulated trail race (EJAP 2025) ([Giovanelli 2019](https://nicolagiovanelli.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/52f25-giovanelli-et-al-ejap-2019.pdf); [IJSPP 2022](https://journals.humankinetics.com/view/journals/ijspp/17/7/ijspp.2021-0274.xml); [EJAP 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00421-025-05881-4)).
  - Use poles on every mountain day and steep ME hike from W11, and on flat-to-rolling segments occasionally to practise stowing them.
- **Race-specific long runs.**
  - Long runs reach 4–7 h with 2,000–3,500 m D+ in the specific phase.
  - Two or three back-to-back weekends in the final 3–7 weeks. Coach consensus; no controlled data ([iRunFar](https://www.irunfar.com/training-for-your-first-ultra)).
  - One 3-day sequence (UA): Ascension, W23.
  - A dress rehearsal of 30–60 % of race distance, 4–6 weeks out (iRunFar predictor workout), on race kit and fueling. This is also W23.
- **Fueling practice.**
  - Targets: 30–60 g/h up to ~2 h, and up to 90 g/h beyond 3 h using 2:1 glucose:fructose. Fluid 450–750 ml/h ([ISSN position stand, Tiller 2019](https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-019-0312-9)).
  - Two weeks of repeated 90 g/h gut challenges cut GI symptoms and malabsorption ([Miall 2018](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/sms.12912); [systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10185635/)). Evidence is mixed in highly trained athletes ([King 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9105618/)), and > 90 g/h is poorly evidenced.
  - Progression: 50–60 g/h on every run > 2 h from W9 → 70 g/h from W13 → 80–90 g/h on every mountain day from W17.
  - Run two concentrated 2-week "gut blocks" (W17–18 and W22–23), with ≥ 3 sessions/week at the target rate, including the race-day products.
- **Night running.** Three sessions:
  - W18: a 2 h headlamp run on known trail.
  - W19 or W23: a mountain day starting at 03:30–04:00.
  - W22: the last hour of a long run after dark.
  - Practise headlamp descents specifically, because the descending limiter is worse at night.

## 4. Volume progression, deloads, taper

- **Hours:** 6.5 h (W1) → 8–9 h (Jan) → 10–12 h (Feb–Mar) → 12–14 h (Apr–May). Peaks in W22 and W23 (W23 counts the Ascension 3-day block; cap it there).
  - Increase ≤ 10 %/week (UA), or 10–30 % in the return phase (OSU) while the base is low.
  - Never raise hours, intensity and vert in the same week.
- **Long run:** 1h30 → 2h30 (Dec) → 3h–3h30 (Jan) → 4h–4h30 (Feb–Mar) → 5h–7h (Apr–May).
  - Add +30 min per block, not per week. Mountain weekends jump +1–1.5 h over the urban long run.
  - The longest single day is 6.5–7 h, about 40–45 % of race time. The 3-day block covers about 70 % of race D+.
- **Weekly D+ / D−:** 700 m → 2,000 m (Jan) → 2,500–3,800 m (Feb–Mar) → 3,500–6,000 m (Apr–May), with D− matched.
  - D− rises ≤ 10–15 %/week except planned mountain spikes. A spike must be preceded by 2–3 weeks of downhill reps.
  - Race density is ~59 m D+/km. Lyon terrain limits normal weeks to about 2,500–3,500 m, so the big vert weeks are mountain weekends plus Pilat.
- **Deload cadence:** 3:1 (UA, D'Haene, RunMotion). Deload weeks run at −35–45 % hours, keep one short quality session and the hill strides, and include a benchmark test.
  - Use 2:1 if calf symptoms recur or Strava/HRV flag poor recovery.
- **Taper (3 weeks after the relay week + 1):**
  - Evidence base: Bosquet 2007 meta-analysis found the best result with 2 weeks at −41–60 % volume and unchanged intensity and frequency ([Bosquet](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Effects-of-tapering-on-performance:-a-Bosquet-Montpetit/a41517ab5fa06b92568b861e2b1aa32b3003d214)). The 2023 meta-analysis found ≤ 21 days, progressive or step, −41–60 % ([PLOS ONE 2023](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0282838)).
  - Prescription: W27 ≈ 75–80 % of peak hours (10–11 h), W28 ≈ 55–60 % (7.5–8 h), W29 ≈ 35–40 % before the race.
  - Keep frequency (5–6 runs) and one short threshold/VO2 touch per week.
  - The last ≥ 4 h day is 20–21 days out (W26, 29–30/05). The last big D− (> 1,500 m) is ≥ 14 days out. The last hard intervals are 9–10 days out.

## 5. Return to run (he is in week 3 of the return on 30/11)

- **Starting volume (Johnston):** start at about 50 % of normal volume after > 4 weeks off, then +5–10 %/week.
- **OSU guideline (≥ 4 weeks off):** after the walk/run phases, add 10–30 %/week and resume normal training at 75–80 % of previous mileage ([OSU](https://wexnermedical.osu.edu/-/media/files/wexnermedical/patient-care/healthcare-services/sports-medicine/education/medical-professionals/other/basic-return-to-running-guideline.pdf)).
- **Hold the progression** if there is sharp pain, pain that builds during the run, or a gait change.
- **Tendon pain-monitoring:** pain ≤ 3/10 during the run (≤ 5/10 tolerated in Silbernagel's model), settling within 24 h, and morning stiffness no worse the next day. If any criterion fails, repeat the previous week's load.
- **Calf/Achilles loading order:** flat easy → frequency → duration → short moderate hill strides → steep *hiking* → uphill threshold → uphill VO2 → hill sprints → downhill reps → long descents. The rehab consensus is flat before uphill and downhill last; uphill sharply raises calf load ([Physio Network](https://www.physio-network.com/blog/calf-strain-rehab/); [MedStar](https://medstarsportphysio.ca/journal/calf-strain-return-to-running)).
  - Don't introduce uphill reps and strides in the same week.
  - Steep running loads the calf–Achilles more than steep hiking. In W1–W6, *hike* anything above about 12 %.
- **Timeline applied** (calendar from 09/11):
  - W1–W2 (weeks 4–5 of return): easy only plus 4–6 hill strides at 6–8 %.
  - W3: first steady Z3 segment (2×10 min, flat or rolling).
  - From W5 (week 8 of return): first uphill VO2 session (5×2 min at 6–10 %, running).
  - W5–W6: hill sprints at 8–12 % grade (not 20 %).
  - W6–W7: first downhill reps.
  - From W9: long descents > 300 m.
  - Each step requires 2 consecutive symptom-free weeks on the calf criteria above.
  - Without strength sessions there is no heel-raise gate, so use a field gate before steep running: 25 single-leg hops or hill strides that are pain-free and symmetrical. Flag this to the athlete. A clinician should look at the calves if soreness lasts beyond W3.
- **Do not lose the base out of caution.** Koop's data say continuity matters more than intensity. Keep 5 runs/week even when the calf forces sessions to be shorter.

## 6. The 6 h relay on 22/05 (W25): demands, preparation, effect on the 100 km

**Demands.** Each runner does about 20 × 2 km loops (+90 m / −90 m), roughly 8.5–9.5 min each, with about 9 min rest. That adds up to:
- **Hard running:** ≈ 3 h total at roughly 95–101 % VT2 HR (≈ CS effort on a hilly loop). The first loops tempt him above VT2.
- **Descending:** ≈ 1,800 m D− at speed, a large eccentric dose.
- **Overall load:** in physiological terms it resembles a very long threshold interval session (Koop TempoRun ×3) or a hard road marathon, *not* an ultra.
- **Fatigue source:** with 1:1 rest, each rep only partly refills W′. The limiter over 20 reps is cumulative glycogen use and quad damage, not VO2max.

There is no controlled literature on this format; the advice is drawn from practitioner and team-relay sources ([LW Coaching duo strategy](https://lwcoaching.com/racing-duo-what-is-the-best-strategy/); [24 h relay field study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3287968/)).

**Preparation (W21–W24)**
- **Weekly relay simulator** on a hilly Lyon loop with similar profile (Sarra/Fourvière, about 2 km / 80–100 m):
  - W21: 6×8 min / 8 min
  - W22: 8×9 min / 9 min
  - W23: no simulator (Ascension block)
  - W24: 12×9 min / 9 min at target loop time
  - Run the first 3 reps at RPE 7.5, not 9. Aim for even or negative splits. The 100 km gets nothing from this session if he blows up on rep 5.
- **Fuelling and recovery:** practise 60–90 g/h and between-loop routines: walk 2 min, drink and eat, layer up, 1–2 min re-warm jog before the handover.
- **Replacement, not addition:** these sessions *replace* the uphill threshold session. They are the threshold block's final form, so total hard sessions stay at two per week, with the other being a long climb or ME.
- **Downhill readiness:** the descending part of the loop must be covered by the downhill-rep progression (≥ 6 weeks of exposure by W25), or the relay will damage his quads more than it should.

**Relay week (W25)**
- Mon off.
- Tue 60 min with 4×3 min at relay effort.
- Wed 60 min Z1.
- Thu 45 min with 4 strides.
- Fri off or 30 min.
- Sat relay.
- Sun off or a 30–45 min walk/jog.
- No long run, no big D−. Week total ≈ 6 h plus the relay (≈ 3 h hard).

**Recovery after (W26)**
- D+1–2: off or a walk.
- D+3–5: 40–60 min Z1, flat.
- D+5: test with 6 hill strides plus a 10 min downhill jog. Quads should be normal.
- D+7 or 8 (Sat 29/05 or Sun 30/05): if the test is fine, the last long mountain day (4–5 h, 2,000–2,500 m, race fueling, poles, Z1–Z2 only). If quads are still sore or HR is suppressed, switch to 3 h easy hilly. The fitness is already built; this run only rehearses.

**Does it help or hurt?**
- **Helps:** it is a big, specific threshold stimulus at the right time for "last intensity", a long fueling rehearsal under stress, and a confidence and mental-toughness boost. The repeated eccentric loops also renew the repeated bout effect 4 weeks out.
- **Hurts:** it costs about 7–10 days of normal training. It displaces the May mountain weekend (hence Ascension, W23). The risk lies in the descending dose and in going out too hard.
- **Net:** neutral to positive *if* (a) pacing is even, (b) the downhill progression has protected the quads, (c) W26 is respected, and (d) nothing new comes afterwards. It must not become a second peak: no mini-taper longer than 4 days, and the 100 km taper does not start until W27.

## Session library

Intensities use VT2 HR = 171 bpm. VAM values are pre-test estimates; recalibrate them with T1. "Not when" lists hard stop rules.

### S1. Recovery run (R)
- **Purpose:** blood flow and volume at near-zero cost.
- **Structure:** 30–45 min flat (quays, parks), < 79 % VT2 (< 135), RPE 2–3.
- **Progression:** unchanged all season.
- **Not when:** the calf limps at the first steps. Walk or bike instead (cross-training was not requested, so prefer rest).

### S2. Endurance run (Z1–Z2), flat or hilly
- **Purpose:** the aerobic base, which makes up most of the training volume.
- **Structure:** 50–90 min at 79–91 % VT2 (135–155), RPE 3–5. On hilly days, run gentle grades and hike above 15 %.
- **Progression:** +5–10 min per block. Hilly versions add 300–600 m D+ by March.
- **Not when:** never skipped for "quality". If short on time, keep this run and drop the quality session.

### S3. Hill strides (Roche)
- **Purpose:** neuromuscular power, economy, and calf/Achilles stiffness with low damage.
- **Structure:** in the 2nd half of an easy run, 4–8 × 15–25 s uphill on 6–10 %. Smooth and fast, about 3k–5k effort (RPE 7–8), not a sprint. Walk or jog down for full recovery (60–90 s).
- **Progression:** 4×15 s (W1) → 8×20 s (W6); 1–3×/week all season, including the taper.
- **Not when:** calf pain > 3/10 during or the morning after. In that case use flat strides or none.

### S4. Hill sprints (UA power)
- **Purpose:** maximal recruitment and tendon stiffness; the running-only stand-in for heavy strength.
- **Structure:** after a 20 min warm-up, 6–10 × 8–10 s *maximal* on 8–12 % (W5–W8), then 15–20 % or stairs (from W9). 2–3 min walk recovery. Stop when distance drops.
- **Progression:** 4 → 10 reps W5–W12; 1×/week in Base and Build, every 10 days in Specific, stopped in W27.
- **Not when:** in the first 4 weeks of the return; with any calf or Achilles symptom; within 48 h after a mountain day.

### S5. Uphill VO2max repeats (Koop RunningIntervals)
- **Purpose:** raise VO2max and the speed at VO2max. Least specific, so it comes first (reverse periodization).
- **Structure:**
  - 20 min warm-up with 3 strides.
  - 5–6 × 3–4 min uphill, *running*, on 6–12 % (Montée de la Sarra, Fourvière, Mont Cindre road).
  - RPE 9. HR ends each rep at ≥ 174 (≥ 102 % VT2). VAM ≈ 1,050–1,250 m/h. 12–24 min total.
  - Recovery equal to the rep, jogging or walking down.
  - 15 min cool-down.
- **Progression:**
  - W5 5×2 min → W6 6×3 min → W7 5×3 min → W9 5×4 min → W10 6×4 min.
  - Maintenance 5×4 min every 10–14 days (W15, W19, W23).
  - Taper versions: W28 5×3 min, W29 4×2 min.
- **Not when:** HR can't reach Z5 by rep 3 (fatigue: stop); within 72 h of a long mountain day; the 8 days before the relay or the race (except the short openers).

### S6. Flat sub-threshold "singles" (Norwegian-style)
- **Purpose:** lift CS and threshold with low muscle damage. Volume of quality.
- **Structure:**
  - 15 min warm-up on the quays.
  - 6×6 min / 1 min, then 5×8 min / 1 min, then 3×12 min / 2 min.
  - 95–98 % VT2 (163–168), RPE 7–8, ≈ 13.3–13.8 km/h. Must feel "controlled": he could do one more rep.
  - 15 min cool-down.
- **Progression:** 36 → 40 → 45 min of work over W13–W16. Afterwards it is absorbed into the relay simulator.
- **Not when:** HR drifts above 170. Slow down; this session is about the ceiling. Not in the same week as two other hard sessions.

### S7. Uphill threshold (Koop TempoRun / UA Z4)
- **Purpose:** sustained climbing power near VT2, and race-grade economy.
- **Structure:**
  - 20 min warm-up.
  - Reps on 8–15 % (run) or 18–25 % (hike with poles): 3×8 min → 3×10 min → 3×12 min → 2×20 min → 3×15 min.
  - 98–101 % VT2 (168–172), RPE 8. VAM ≈ 950–1,150 (run) / 1,050–1,250 (hike).
  - Recovery half the rep, easy descent or walk. Use a long descent between reps only once downhill tolerance is established.
- **Progression:** total 24 → 45 min W9–W18. Taper: 4×5 min (W27).
- **Not when:** the calf is reactive (switch to hiking); in the week after a mountain weekend that left DOMS.

### S8. Steady-state long climb (Koop SSR / UA Z3)
- **Purpose:** race-specific climbing intensity. Doubles as unloaded ME. Raises the HR or VAM he can hold for hours.
- **Structure:**
  - 15–20 min warm-up.
  - Continuous climb or repeats: 2×20 min → 2×30 min → 45 min → 2×30–45 min.
  - Monts d'Or (300–450 m climbs), repeated Fourvière stairs, Pilat (1,000 m climbs).
  - 91–95 % VT2 (155–163), RPE 6–7. VAM 800–950. Hike > 18 % with poles.
  - Jog or walk down between climbs; descend with good technique.
- **Progression:** W14 → W22. Also embed it in long mountain days, hours 3–5 ("fatigued steady").
- **Not when:** dehydrated or under-fuelled. Fuel 60+ g/h, because this session is also gut training.

### S9. ME steep power hike (UA muscular endurance, running-only form)
- **Purpose:** local leg endurance for 5,700 m D+, hiking economy and pole technique.
- **Structure:**
  - On ≥ 20–35 % trails or long staircases (Gourguillon/Fourvière steps, Croix-Rousse stairs), or a treadmill at 15 %+ if travelling.
  - 3×10 min → 4×10 min → 3×15 min → 2×20 min of steep hiking.
  - Legs burn, breathing stays conversational: HR ≈ 85–93 % VT2 (145–160), RPE 6–7 legs / 4–5 breathing.
  - Walk down easily between reps; the descent counts as gentle eccentric work.
  - Optional pack of 5–10 % body weight (≈ 4–7 kg of water bottles, dumped before descending) in W13–W18.
- **Progression:** 1×/week W9–W20, 8–12 weeks. From W21 it turns into unloaded steep climbs inside long runs.
- **Not when:** breathing becomes the limiter (slope too gentle or pace too high); with Achilles symptoms (steep dorsiflexion); with a pack heavier than 10 % body weight; in the last 6 weeks.

### S10. Downhill repeats (eccentric conditioning, repeated bout effect)
- **Purpose:** quad and calf eccentric tolerance, protection from race-day damage, and descending technique at speed.
- **Structure:**
  - At the end of an easy or hilly run, on smooth trail or dirt road (Monts d'Or tracks, Parc de la Tête d'Or slopes are too gentle; use Sarra or the Monts d'Or forest roads).
  - Reps: 4–6 × 30–60 s on −6–10 % (W6–W8) → 6–8 × 1–2 min on −8–12 % (W9–W13) → 8–12 × 1.5–2 min or 2×5 min continuous on −10–15 % (W14–W22).
  - Controlled-fast effort (RPE 6–7; HR is irrelevant). Cadence high, at preferred +0–5 %. Short contacts, eyes 3–5 m ahead, arms wide.
  - Walk back up; the climb is the recovery.
- **Progression:** one variable at a time (grade → duration → speed); 1×/week; ≥ 48 h before any quality session.
- **Not when:**
  - Calf or Achilles pain (descents load the plantar flexors eccentrically too; their MVC falls 15–25 % after downhill running).
  - DOMS from the previous session not yet resolved.
  - In the 10 days before the race.
  - As a *new* stimulus after W24.

### S11. Long continuous descent
- **Purpose:** lasting quad protection for long Alpine descents (1,000–1,500 m at a time).
- **Structure:**
  - Inside a long run or mountain day: 20–60 min continuous descent on −8–15 % (altitude-trail protocol: 30–60 min at −8–12 %), e.g. Pilat crest → Pélussin (≈ 900–1,000 m), or Alpine descents on mountain weekends.
  - Race effort, *not* faster. Steady cadence. Poles packed or used for balance.
- **Progression:**
  - First 300 m (W9) → 600 m (W11) → 900–1,000 m (W15, W19) → 1,500 m+ (W22–W23).
  - Once every 10–14 days during peak (iRunFar), to keep the repeated bout effect.
- **Not when:** without at least 3 prior weeks of S10; ≤ 14 days before the race with > 1,500 m D−; the week before the relay.

### S12. Technical descent skill
- **Purpose:** fix below-average descending through skill, at low metabolic and eccentric cost.
- **Structure:**
  - In an easy run, 6–10 × 20–60 s on rocky or rooty technical trail (Monts d'Or single-track, Pilat chirats).
  - Focus on one cue per rep: quick feet, hips over feet, look ahead, arms out, pick lines.
  - Repeat the same segment to measure progress (Strava segment). Include 2–3 headlamp sessions (S17).
  - 6×200 m fast technical descents (altitude-trail) once descent conditioning is established (from W13).
- **Progression:** 1×/week from W9. Extend to longer technical sections on mountain weekends.
- **Not when:** on wet limestone with fatigue; after a hard long run (falls).

### S13. Long run, progressive / durability
- **Purpose:** durability (the efficiency drop per hour), fat oxidation, gut training, time on feet.
- **Structure:**
  - Hilly Lyon or Monts d'Or, Z1–Z2 (79–91 % VT2), hike > 15 %.
  - From W13, finish with 30–45 min Z3 (155–163) or one steady climb ("fatigued quality").
  - Fuel 50–60 → 70 → 90 g/h. Fluid 450–750 ml/h. Sodium per sweat rate.
- **Progression:** 1h45 (W1) → 3h (W9) → 4h (W14) → 5h (W21). +30 min per block, −30–40 % in deload weeks.
- **Not when:** with a calf flare (cut it short and stay flat). Don't run the Z3 finish if HR drift > 8–10 % in the Z2 part: that is the durability signal, so log it and back off.

### S14. Mountain day (race-specific)
- **Purpose:** the most specific session: 2,000–3,500 m D+/D−, race terrain, kit, poles, fueling, pacing discipline.
- **Structure:**
  - 4–7 h. Run only where the race would be run; hike > 18–20 % at VAM 650–800 m/h (Z2, ≤ 152 bpm on long climbs).
  - One climb at Z3 in the second half from W17. Race-pace long descents.
  - 80–90 g/h from W17. Full mandatory kit.
- **Progression:** W7 3h30 / 1,500 m → W11 4h30 / 2,200 m → W15 5h30 / 2,800 m → W19 6h / 3,000 m (night start) → W23 6h30 / 2,800–3,000 m dress rehearsal → W26 4–5h / 2,000–2,500 m last long.
- **Not when:** ≤ 20 days before the race at full length; 7 days after the relay if the downhill test fails.

### S15. Back-to-back (B2B) day 2
- **Purpose:** running on pre-fatigued legs and low glycogen without doing one dangerous mega-run.
- **Structure:** the day after the long or mountain day, 1h30–3h at Z1–Z2 on hilly terrain. Include 20–30 min of descending on tired legs (technique under fatigue). Fuel normally.
- **Progression:** light from W13 (1h30); 2h in W17–W21; 3h in W19, W22 and W23 (day 3 of the Ascension block). 2–4 real B2B weekends in the final 3–7 weeks.
- **Not when:** day 1 left joint or tendon pain (not just muscle fatigue); in deload weeks; after W26.

### S16. Dress rehearsal / predictor
- **Purpose:** test race pacing, kit, food and poles over 30–60 % of the distance (iRunFar predictor workout), 4–6 weeks out.
- **Structure:** W23 Fri (07/05): about 40 km / 2,500–3,000 m in the Alps, ideally on race-like or race-course terrain. Run at *planned race effort* (Z1–Z2, climbs ≤ 152), not faster. Race breakfast, race products at 80–90 g/h, race shoes.
- **Progression:** single event, with lessons re-tested in W26.
- **Not when:** ≤ 3 weeks out; if the snowline makes the course terrain unsafe (use Pilat/Chartreuse at lower altitude).

### S17. Night run
- **Purpose:** headlamp descending, sleep-deprived fueling, mental rehearsal.
- **Structure:** 2h Z1 on known trail with a headlamp, including 3–4 technical descents (W18). Mountain day starting at 03:30–04:00 (W19 or W23). Finish the last 45 min of a long run after dark (W22).
- **Progression:** 3 sessions.
- **Not when:** on new trail alone; in ice or snow without spikes.

### S18. Relay simulator
- **Purpose:** prepare for 20 × ~9 min at near-CS on a hilly loop with 9 min rest. Also the final form of the threshold block.
- **Structure:**
  - 2 km loop with ~80–100 m D+ (Sarra/Fourvière), 20 min warm-up.
  - 6×8 min / 8 min (W21) → 8×9 min / 9 min (W22) → 12×9 min / 9 min (W24).
  - Target: 96–101 % VT2 (165–172), RPE 8, even splits. The first 3 reps are deliberately at 95 % VT2.
  - Rest: walk 2 min, eat/drink (60–90 g/h overall), layers, 1–2 min re-warm.
- **Progression:** as above. Record loop times and HR to set relay pacing (start at the W24 median loop time + 2–3 %).
- **Not when:** the week after a mountain weekend with DOMS (the loop's descents add up); within 9 days of the relay at full length (W25 gets only the 4×3 min opener).

### S19. Pre-race opener
- **Purpose:** sharpen without fatigue.
- **Structure:**
  - Before the relay (Tue W25): 15 min warm-up + 4×3 min at relay effort / 3 min + 15 min cool-down.
  - Before the 100 km (Tue W29): 4×2 min at Z4b uphill / 2 min + 4 strides; Thu 30 min with 3 strides.
- **Not when:** never longer; never add reps "because it feels good".

### S20. Benchmark tests
- **T1, uphill calibration:** in a deload week, a known 20–30 min climb (e.g. Mont Verdun or a Pilat road).
  - Return phase (W4): 20 min fixed at 150 bpm, measure VAM.
  - From W8: 30 min all-out TT. Average HR over the last 20 min ≈ VT2 check; VAM sets the S5/S7 targets.
- **HR-drift test** (W6, W16): 60 min at fixed pace near VT1. Drift < 5 % confirms the VT1 estimate (UA AeT test).
- **Durability test** (W12, W20, W24): during a 3 h run at a set pace and HR, repeat the same 2 km flat segment at the same HR at 0:30, 1:30 and 2:30. Pace loss per hour = durability. Target: −4.4 %/h moving toward −2 %/h.
- **Downhill tolerance test** (D+5 after the relay, W26): 10 min controlled descent + 6 hill strides. Quads pain-free and symmetrical is the go signal for S14.

## Main uncertainties

- Koop's book was not available. His seasonal VO2 → tempo → specific sequence comes from his articles and secondary summaries. Roche's Trail Runner workouts were paywalled; their detail comes from secondary summaries.
- There is no study on a 6 h repeated-loop relay 4 weeks before a 100 km. Section 6 is reasoned from the physiology (threshold-session load plus ~1,800 m of fast D−).
- There is no validated downhill "dose". The progression is built from the repeated-bout-effect studies and coach practice.
- Back-to-back timing is coach consensus only.
- Leaving out strength work removes the best-evidenced durability tool. The running-only substitutes (S4, S9, S10) are plausible but less proven.
